KERYX is the live operator console a Brazilian church volunteer runs for 90 minutes from a dark booth: lyrics, Bible passages, videos and announcements go out to several screens while 300 people watch the result on a 4-meter wall. The system exists so that every screen, module and future feature keeps three things readable at a glance — what is on air, what goes next, whether every output is connected — and makes the wrong action hard and the right one instant. Dark only. Colour means state and nothing else.

## Principles

- **Programa / Prévia discipline.** A single click never reaches the screens: it prepares (`prev`). What is on air (`air`) changes only on a deliberate act: `Enviar ao ar`, Enter or Ctrl+Enter, a double click on the item, a drag onto the Programa, or ←/→ moving the slide already on air. Typing never sends. Design every new feature so that this stays true; the full rules are in `docs/design-system.md` › Comportamento.
- **Colour is state.** `air` red = on air, `prev` green = prepared, `rh` blue = rehearsal (outputs disarmed), `warn` amber = warning or failure. `brand` gold is the KERYX identity: the logo mark, the active environment tab, the focus ring and ordinary primary actions (Salvar, Novo culto), never a live state. Everything else is one of four warm greys. If a colour is not telling the operator a state, remove it.
- **Dense, small, quiet.** 11px telemetry, 12px UI, 13–14px content, nothing larger except the live steps (`live-title` 16px for what is on air) and `clock` / `countdown`. Bare glyphs, no boxes around icons, no badges, no pills, no inline help. The actions that change what the audience sees (Tela preta, Logo, Limpar, Encerrar) are one chip of glyphs in the top bar, named by their tooltips and aria-labels (owner decision 2026-10-10, replacing the named Telas group). Explanations live in tooltips and empty states.
- **One rhythm.** A 4px grid, 28px rows and controls, 8px gutters between modules, 8px radius on controls, 14px on panels. Hover and state changes take 120ms ease-out; a slide change on the screens uses the church's transition (Configurações › Aparência).

## Content fundamentals

Write Brazilian Portuguese, never European forms: *tela*, *salvar*, *arquivo*, *excluir*, *cronômetro*, *celular*. Address the operator as *você* only when unavoidable; prefer the imperative (*Enviar ao ar*, *Pôr na prévia*, *Localizar…*). Sentence case everywhere except section headers, which the `section` style sets uppercase. No exclamation marks on the console, no emoji, no jokes while something is on air.

Use these words exactly and never a synonym: **Programa, Prévia, Próximo, Travar telas, Destravar, Enviar ao ar, Pôr na prévia, Ir para o item, Voltar ao anterior, Tela preta, Mostrar logo, Limpar texto, Encerrar, Telas, Ver, Densidade, Confortável, Compacta, Setlist, Biblioteca, Letras, Mídia, Avisos, Camadas, Tema, Fundo, Linha do tempo, Saídas, Conectada, Reconectando, Desconectada, Fechada, Reabrir, Esquecer.** The rundown is the **Setlist** (the canvas's older word, "Roteiro", is retired).

Captions follow one pattern, middle dots as separators, counts as `2/5`: `Saídas · 5 conectadas`, `Saídas · 1 com problema`, `Nenhuma saída aberta`, `Palco desconectada às 19:42`, `Reconectando… tentativa 2`, `+7 min atrasado`. The live column's monitors set the caption in parts instead of one line: the state word (`PROGRAMA`, `PRÉVIA`), the title, and the position (`2/5`) in small type; beside the Programa's caption, `em 3 telas` says how many screens show it (amber when fewer than expected, `em nenhuma tela` when none). Times are `19:46`, durations `4:30`, planned-versus-actual `3:10 de 4:00`. Numbers are always tabular (the `sans` family is loaded with `tnum`).

A warning names the thing and the way out in one line: `Arquivo não encontrado · Localizar…`, `Fonte "Bebas Neue" não instalada · usando Geist`. Confirmation dialogs say what stays on air: *"O que está no ar continua no ar: Firme na rocha, slide 2. Depois de armar, Enviar ao ar muda as 5 telas."*

## Colour

Grounds, from the window out: `bg-0` is the window, `bg-1` every module, `bg-2` fields, popovers and hover, `bg-3` pressed or selected. These four greys and the two hairlines (`line`, `line-strong`) are the whole neutral palette; do not add a fifth step. Text is `fg-1` for content and the row in hand, `fg-2` for UI labels and glyphs at rest, `fg-3` for metadata, headers and placeholders. All three pass 4.5:1 on all three surfaces they are allowed on; never set text in `line-strong` or dimmer.

State colours come in four tokens each — the mark (`air`), the text (`air-fg`), the fill (`air-fill`) and the wash (`air-tint`) — and the same for `prev`, `rh` and `warn` (whose only fill is the PiP label; use `on-warn` on it). Marks are frames, bars and dots; text tokens are the lighter variants that read on `bg-1`; fills are rare — `Enviar ao ar`, *Destravar* on the lock banner and nothing else; a tint is a 10% wash behind a row and only ever appears together with its 2px bar on the row's left edge (`bar`). Text on a fill is `on-state`.

Red and green are distinguishable by lightness (4.7:1 and 5.8:1 on `bg-1`, text variants 6.7:1 and 9.4:1) and always carry a word as well: `PROGRAMA`, `PRÉVIA`, `Conectada`, `Reconectando`. Never signal a state with colour alone.

`brand` gold (`#C19959`) marks identity and ordinary interaction, sparingly — one or two gold cues per region: the KERYX mark, the 2px underline of the active environment tab (KERYX | Bíblia), the 2px focus ring (5.5:1 on `bg-3`), the bars of a report chart, and the gold button `kx-btn-brand` for non-live primary actions (Salvar, Salvar aviso, Novo culto, Importar). The gold button's label is `on-brand` (6.7:1); hover is `brand-hover`, pressed `brand-pressed` (4.7:1, never as text on `bg-2`/`bg-3`), disabled goes neutral (`bg-3`, `fg-3`), and its focus ring turns `fg-1` so it shows on the fill. `brand-tint` is a 10% wash for the rare neutral selection that needs a gold edge. Gold never recolours Programa, Prévia, Enviar ao ar, Ao vivo, Ensaio, an output fault or a warning, and never appears on a projected screen.

Projected screens are a different world: `screen-bg` black, `screen-fg` white text with the `screen-text` shadow, the church logo in a corner. Console tokens never leak onto a screen and screen styles never appear on the console.

## Typography

One family, `sans` (Geist, Inter as fallback, shipped with the app, never loaded from the internet, with `tnum`). Twelve console styles, three screen styles:
- **Everyday:** `meta` 11/16 for telemetry (times, counts, key, length: never the name of an item that is on air or prepared); `section` 11/16 500 uppercase with 0.06em tracking for module headers; `ui` 12/16 and `ui-strong` 12/16 500 for controls; `content` 13/18 for titles in lists; `content-lg` 14/20 for slide and verse text.
- **Live information** (it gets its own steps so it can be found at a glance): `live-cap` 12/16 600 uppercase for the state word of a monitor, in the state's colour; `live-title` 16/22 600 for what is on air, once per screen; `next-title` 14/20 500 for what is prepared and what comes next; `alert-title` 13/18 600 for what failed in an output banner.
- **Time:** `clock` 20/24 and `countdown` 28/32.

On the console nothing is larger than 14px except `live-title` (16px) and those two. Weight is 400 or 500 for everyday text; 600 belongs to the live steps and to projected lyrics and the cover.

Screens use `display` (the same family at heavier weight): `screen-lyrics`, `screen-verse`, `screen-ref`, sized in container units of the monitor so a preview at 320px wide and the real 1920px screen keep the same proportions.

## Spacing, radii, layout

`space-1` (4px) is the unit; controls and rows are `row` (28px) tall (list rows are `row-comfortable`, 32px, in the Confortável density), the top bar `topbar` (40px, on `bg-0` with no panel: the search is a round `bg-1` field, the bell and the Telas group are round `bg-1` chips), gutters between modules `gutter` (8px), module headers 32px with `space-3` on the left. Buttons and rows' actions are at least 24 × 24px (WCAG 2.2 target size; the smoke checks the main console); the glyph inside stays 16px. Not yet raised: the small segmented switches inside panels (Camadas, the player's layer picker), 20px tall. Horizontal padding inside a row is `space-3` on the left (it lines up with the module header) and `space-2` on the right, inside a control `space-2`; popovers and menus pad `space-3`; dialogs `space-4`. Radii: `radius-control` on anything you click or type into, on slide rows and banners, `radius-panel` on modules, popovers and dialogs, `radius-round` on 6px state dots, the seek handle and the top bar's search and chips.

The console is a grid of modules, in three width tiers measured on the grid itself (not the window): **wide** (≥ 1400px) Biblioteca | Slides | the live column | Setlist; **medium** (1100–1399px) the Setlist moves into the Biblioteca's tabs; **narrow** (< 1100px, down to the 960 × 600 window minimum) Biblioteca over Slides in one column beside the live column. The live column (Programa, then a reserved player slot, then the Prévia with Enviar ao ar, then the Próximo line) is the same at every size and never stacks below anything; nothing ever scrolls, in either direction.

Every module has a 32px header (`section` style, collapse glyph, module actions), can be resized with a splitter, collapsed to its header, or hidden and restored from **Ver** (the eye glyph beside the bell: Biblioteca, Slides, Camadas, Setlist, Linha do tempo, plus *Densidade*, *Restaurar layout* and *Atalhos do teclado*). A splitter shows a grip on hover and on keyboard focus; Tab reaches it, ←/→ (↑/↓ for a horizontal one) resize in 16px steps, Enter or double-click restores. Columns that cannot be resized are separated by a plain `kx-gap`, with no grip. Camadas is a section above the Setlist in the wide tier's right column (a horizontal splitter between them); narrower, it is a drawer opened from the Programa's header, over the source columns and never over the Programa. Layouts and the density are saved per operator.

## States

A row's hover actions (Favoritar, Editar, Adicionar) never cover the middle of the row, because a click there prepares: in the Biblioteca's song list (`kx-acts-list`) they show their names from 300px of row width up and shrink to glyphs below it (the star, a pencil, a plus), leaving at least 16px between the actions and the middle at the column's 200px minimum.

A highlighted row is a 2px bar on its left edge in the state colour plus the matching 10% tint; the title takes the state's text colour. Selected-but-neutral rows use `bg-3`. Done items take `dim` opacity on the whole row. Disabled controls take `dim` and lose hover. Hover lightens the glyph (`fg-2` → `fg-1`) and adds a `bg-2` background; pressed is `bg-3`. Focus is the `brand` ring, inset so it never grows the control.

The Programa has one title: its panel's header is the live caption (the state word, what is on air at 16px, its position; *em N telas* and the actions on the right), and its monitor has no caption row of its own. The word is `air-fg` only while something is on air, grey when nothing is (*nada*), `rh-fg` when locked (*Programa · travado*) and `warn-fg` while the projector's monitor is not connected (the banner says why). The Programa monitor has **no frame while it is simply on air** (owner decision 2026-10-10: the red lives in the word and in the on-air slide's bar); a frame appears only for a state that needs attention: blue `rh` when locked, amber `warn` on a failure. The Prévia keeps its green frame and its caption on the monitor. The amber hatch belongs to a Prévia whose file is missing (*Arquivo não encontrado*). With the screens locked the Programa still shows the real screen: they are the same image.

The *Alerta rápido* is the one control that bypasses Prévia: its field is captioned *vai direto às telas*, and while an alert is showing it becomes a `kx-alert` line (warn bar, warn tint, elapsed time, *Tirar*); on the screens it is a thin `warn` bar across the top in `on-warn` ink. Tools (Relógio, Cronômetro, Timer, Texto livre) are `kx-tool` ghost buttons that go through Prévia like any item and render on screen as `kx-screen-tool` (a label above a tabular figure at 14% of the screen width). Per-output routing (Letra, Letra + cifra, Playback, Cifra, Avisos) and the transport's *Toca em* are one setting, shown and changed with `kx-route`.

Banners are one 28px line across the grid, tinted, with a 2px top rule in the state colour: blue for `TELAS TRAVADAS — nada novo vai às telas` (with *Destravar*), amber for an output that failed (its title in `alert-title`, the detail in grey, the way out as buttons: *Reabrir*, *Ver saídas*, *Esquecer*). Output banners never stack with each other: one at a time, an output reconnecting on a missing monitor first (it has Tela preta and Mostrar logo beside it), else the destination that dropped most recently, with "e mais N" for the others. The blue lock banner is separate and can sit with one.

An output is in one of these states, always with a word: *Conectada*, *Reconectando…*, *Desconectada às 19:42* (a network screen that left), *Fechada às 19:30* (a window that closed), *Fechada* (never opened this session), *Conectada · travada* (screens locked). The status in the top bar says *Saídas · N conectadas*, *Saídas · N com problema* (amber) or *Nenhuma saída aberta* (amber, when live with nothing open), and *Telas travadas* (blue) while locked, unless something is wrong; a green dot never sits beside zero.

## Iconography

Bare glyphs from the KERYX set (`components/bundle.js`, `window.KERYX.icon(name)`, SVGs under `assets/Icons`): 24-unit grid drawn for 16px, 1.5 stroke, round caps and joins, no fill, no box, border or background around them (the one exception: the top bar's Ver, bell and screen actions sit in round `bg-1` chips, because the bar itself has no panel). A glyph sits at `fg-2` and lightens to `fg-1` on hover; a glyph next to a label that already says the same thing is removed. The way back from a full-screen view is the one outlined button with a label, *Console*, first in the view's first header (`BackBtn`). Shortcuts appear in tooltips (`Tela preta · B`), not printed on buttons. Two prints remain from earlier rounds and are an open decision: `Ctrl+Enter` beside *Enviar ao ar*, and `Enter` on the Bíblia's send button.

## Motion

One transition — `duration-fast` with `ease-out` — on background-color, color, border-color and opacity for hover, focus, selection and state changes. Nothing slides, scales or fades in on the console; popovers appear in place. A slide change on the projected screens (and the console's monitors, which are those screens) uses the transition the church chose in Configurações › Aparência — Esmaecer, Deslizar or Corte, 120 to 600 ms — and an item may choose its own in Camadas; the console's own UI motion is unchanged. `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` removes the transition.
